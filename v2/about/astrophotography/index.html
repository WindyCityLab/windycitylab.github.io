A telescope in Texas.
A window on the night sky.

I operate a deep-sky imaging system at Starfront Observatories from my home in Chicago. Astrophotography brings together things I enjoy: astronomy, patient observation, and the challenge of making a whole system work.

Behind an image

Light, tracking,
and a little patience.

01

Capture the light

A William Optics RedCat 91 refractor and cooled ZWO ASI6200MM Pro monochrome camera capture deep-sky objects through broadband and narrowband filters. The ZWO AM5N mount and guide camera keep the system tracking through long exposures.

02

Coordinate the night

NINA runs the imaging sequence, PHD2 guides the telescope, and ASTAP checks its pointing. Focus, framing, filter changes, and the end of the session are coordinated remotely.

03

Plan for the unexpected

A Raspberry Pi and network power switch provide a recovery path separate from the imaging computer. Captured files move to NAS storage, where I can retain and process them independently.

The engineering is part of the pleasure.

I also build Starfront, my own software for planning, reviewing, stacking, and processing these imaging sessions.
